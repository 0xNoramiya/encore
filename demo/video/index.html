When someone can no longer tell you what they love, their taste graph still can.

Built on Qloo’s taste graph · encore-qloo.fly.dev

A comfort and connection tool, not a medical device or treatment.

Dorothy grew up in Brooklyn.
In nineteen fifty-three, Dorothy saw Roman Holiday twice
and sang Sinatra on the front stoop.
Today, Dorothy lives with dementia.
And some days, there's no way to ask what still brings joy.
Families are told: play music from their youth.
But 'oldies' is a decade
not a person.
A playlist has never met Dorothy.
Encore starts with a few memories.
A first name.
A birth year.
A hometown.
A few things Dorothy loved.
If a title is ambiguous, Encore asks.
Then an agent goes to work on Qloo's taste graph.
Music, film, TV, books, and places near home
all from the years Dorothy was ten to thirty.
Every step is a real Qloo call you can open.
Dorothy's world.
Tony Bennett.
Funny Girl.
A slice from Joe's Pizza.
And every pick says why.
We gave the same story to the same AI, without Qloo.
It asked Dorothy to remember, five times out of eight
and mostly repeated what we told it.
Encore found seven new things.
Every one traceable.
Never a quiz.
Session Mode is calm.
One thing at a time, big type, a gentle voice.
The caregiver taps how Dorothy responds
and if something upsets Dorothy, it stops, and a favourite takes its place.
Then Encore learns.
What made Dorothy sing along now steers the taste graph
and the next session finds more like it.
In Roberto's first language.
For a whole room of residents.
With a report the family and care team can keep.
